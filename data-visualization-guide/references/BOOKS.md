## Foundational works referenced indirectly

These predate or sit outside the O'Reilly catalog search but are the bedrock the books above build
on, and are cited by name in the reference files:

- **Edward Tufte**, *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information* — data-ink ratio, chartjunk,
  lie factor, small multiples, graphical integrity.
- **William Cleveland & Robert McGill** — the graphical-perception accuracy ranking and
  "banking to 45°."
- **Stephen Few**, *Information Dashboard Design* / *Show Me the Numbers* — dashboards, bullet
  graphs, sparklines, grey-by-default restraint, tables-vs-graphs.
- **Ben Shneiderman** — "overview first, zoom and filter, details on demand."
- **Okabe & Ito** — colorblind-safe qualitative palette.

## How this skill was grounded

The book list was assembled by searching the O'Reilly Learning catalog for the canonical
data-visualization literature (queries across communication, chart design, perception, color,
dashboards, storytelling, and individual authors). The skill encodes the principles these sources
agree on; where they differ (e.g. Tufte's minimalism vs. Cairo's and Setlur/Cogley's defense of
functional decoration), the reference files note the tension rather than pretending consensus.
